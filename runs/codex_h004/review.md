**[MAJOR] Precision depends on discordance and the unit of replication.** The subset and seed were frozen. I found no prospective power calculation supporting the “power-verified subset” provision. For scale, at 200 independent pairs with a true +5 pp effect and 14% discordance, an exact two-sided McNemar test has about 40% power at .05 and 30% at .025. At 23.5% discordance those figures are 26% and 18%. These sensitivity calculations integrate over the binomial discordant count and use the observed T1–C0 and T1–C4 discordance rates as scenarios. They do not estimate the joint power of the success criteria. One session per track precludes run-level uncertainty. Resampling individual OOD problems also omits between-template uncertainty. With sparse changes, percentile intervals can mislead: T2 lost five OOD items and gained none, giving [−10, −1] pp and exact p = .0625. Report both. ### 3. Fairness and mechanism

**[MAJOR] The T1–C4 gap has two observed components.** T1 scored 54.5%, C0 51.5% and C4 weights 47.0%. The 7.5 pp difference comprises +3.0 pp for T1 and −4.5 pp for C4 relative to C0. The latter interval is [−11.5, +2.5] pp (p = .272). Attributing the gap to C4 degradation alone overstates the evidence. T1 exceeded row-permuted C5 by one net answer (9 gains, 8 losses, +0.5 pp [−3.5, +4.5], p = 1). T2 scored 3.5 pp below norm-matched C5 [−8.5, +1.0]. These comparisons fail to establish donor-specific benefit or equivalence. C5 preserves aspects of donor structure, and T2's C5 retains the activation-selected head and offset. It tests the donor-value direction conditional on that localization. Each shuffle has one seed. **[MAJOR] The controls answer different questions.** Shared items, decoding and scoring make the checkpoint comparisons fair. Their whole-model relative edit norms were .01003 and .00441 versus .0000395 and .000177. Model access, search procedures and edit magnitudes differ.
